# Campaign rules — draft for legal review

**Status: DRAFT. Not legal advice. Not published terms.**
This document exists so a Canadian lawyer and accountant can review the actual
rules Common Record intends to operate under before the first dollar is
accepted or the first request is filed. Nothing here binds anyone yet.

It covers the Phase 2 contribution model in `docs/roadmap.md`: one-time CAD
contributions toward the defined cost of acquiring and publishing public
records. It does not describe donations to a charity, investments, or a
refundable wallet.

## 1. What a contribution is

- A contribution funds a specific campaign's stated acquisition budget:
  application fees, processing fees, postage, and a stated share of preparation
  labour.
- Contributions are restricted funds. They buy no control over request wording,
  scope, findings, timing, or publication.
- Contributors receive the public outputs everyone receives. Contributors
  receive no preferential access to records, datasets, or drafts.
- No charitable tax receipts are issued unless a registered charity is
  responsible for them. Until then, receipts acknowledge payment only.
- No minimum beyond a practical floor (proposed: $5) to avoid processing
  transactions that cost more than they raise.

## 2. Overfunding

- If contributions exceed the stated budget, the campaign stops accepting
  contributions at 105% of budget.
- Surplus is allocated in this order:
  1. fee overruns and appeal costs for the same campaign;
  2. acquisition costs of the same campaign's follow-up requests (narrowing,
     clarification, appeal);
  3. publication and dataset work for the same campaign;
  4. only if the campaign is fully complete: the next campaign in the same
     subject area and jurisdiction, disclosed on the original campaign page.
- Surplus is never returned to a general fund or used for unrelated operating
  costs without a note on the campaign page.

## 3. Underfunding and campaign failure

- If a campaign does not reach its minimum funding target within its stated
  window, no requests are filed and contributions are refunded in full minus
  unavoidable payment-processing costs.
- If a campaign is funded but an institution refuses, returns nothing, or
  exhausts appeal, the campaign page states plainly what was spent
  (application fees, processing fees, appeal fees) and what happened.
- Remaining funds after a failed campaign are refunded pro rata unless the
  contributor chose a stated alternative (surplus allocation above) at
  contribution time.

## 4. Fee overruns after filing

- Each fee estimate above the application fee is posted to the campaign page
  before it is paid.
- If the estimate exceeds the remaining campaign funds, the request is paused
  and one of three paths is chosen and recorded: narrow the request, dispute
  the fee, or fund the overrun from surplus rules above.

## 5. Withdrawal and narrowing

- A request may be narrowed or withdrawn only by an authorized operator, with
  the reason recorded as a public timeline event.
- Narrowing is preferred over withdrawal when it preserves a useful partial
  answer.

## 6. Refunds and chargebacks

- Refunds are issued: for underfunded campaigns, for amounts charged in error,
  and when a filing route turns out to be unavailable.
- Refunds are not issued for dissatisfaction with the content of records
  released by an institution.
- Chargebacks are recorded as separate ledger events. A contributor whose
  chargeback is upheld may have future contributions declined.
- Financial history is never rewritten in place; corrections are new ledger
  entries.

## 7. Publication and records

- Everything acquired is published: released records, normalized data,
  methodology, documented gaps, and the complete public timeline.
- Redactions applied by institutions are documented. Common Record applies no
  redactions of its own to released records except narrowly for requester
  safety, with a public note.
- Datasets are versioned. Corrections are new versions with change notes.

## 8. Privacy

- Requester identity and contact details held by Common Record are private.
  The legal requester is a named individual or organization that has consented
  to that role.
- Contributor identities are not published by default. Anonymous public
  attribution is supported. Payment-provider requirements for payer records
  take precedence over anonymity.
- Operator notes, correspondence, and internal deliberations remain private
  until an operator publishes a redacted public event.
- Personal information is retained no longer than the published
  records-retention schedule requires (to be drafted separately).

## 9. Questions reviewers must answer before launch

1. Which legal entity receives contributions, and under what consumer
   protection regime?
2. Are these contributions subject to any tax, and what records must be kept?
3. Are the refund, surplus, and failure rules enforceable as written, and what
   must change?
4. What unclaimed-funds obligations apply to refunds never claimed?
5. Does publishing refusal and fee-dispute correspondence raise any privacy
   problem?
